Playbook v0: every proposal, explained, for your approval

This page covers every change proposed in proposals.md, plus the conflicts I found while writing it up, the fixes to the research, and the options for what comes next. Nothing here has been applied. Mark each item approve, change or reject. Your marks save in this browser, and the button at the bottom copies them out as text.

The one thing to know before marking anything. Loid's benchmark (calibration.md §2.5) needs a baseline run (B0) on today's prompts before any proposal that changes how Luna or Mika behave is applied. Otherwise there is no way to tell whether the playbook improved the eye or just changed it. Approving an item here means "apply it after B0", not "apply it now". The sequence is at the end (§9).

2. Conflicts that need a ruling first

I found these while writing this review. They are not in proposals.md. Each one blocks or changes an item further down.

G1

Voice approvals: my rule contradicts the Gnomon spec

Rec. change ruleS
What changes
rules.yaml → voice-confirm-and-echo-irreversible, and board E12.
Why
The rule lets an irreversible action be approved by voice after a spoken confirm. docs/specs/gnomon.md (S22-M1 / A23-2) says the voice principal is "never approve … never a decision by speech". The spec is a ruling and it wins. NN/g's voice research (users don't retry failed capabilities; voice suits single-step commands) supports the spec. NN/g
Example
Before (rule)
"Deploy to prod, say confirm." → approved by voice, echoed on screen
After (rule renamed voice-opens-never-decides)
"Two cards pending; the deploy one is gate-class. Opening it."
→ card focused on screen; decision needs presence (Touch ID)
Benefit
Founder: one source of truth for voice authority. Eye: Mika stops passing designs the kernel would refuse.
Cost / risk
None. Narrower voice scope at launch, which the research favours anyway.
Depends on
Nothing.
Measured by
Not benchmarkable yet (no voice items). Tracked as an outcome under §3.2 once voice ships.
G2

Rec. relabel DE-02bS
What changes

Why

Claude Code itself shows "Thinking", and Gnomon has a persona. Loid's DE-02 seeds "Status line says Thinking…" as a minor defect under topics.R7. If the tell skips it, the benchmark scores a correct critic as missing it.
Options
(a) Recommended: relabel DE-02b as waits-show-honest-progress. The defect is "Thinking…" with no factual action for over a second, not the word itself. (b) Keep the label and add "Thinking" to the tell's regex. (c) Drop the seed.
Example

Passes under (a)
⠋ Thinking · read 4 files · editing BillingForm.tsx
Benefit
Keeps persona voice in Gnomon and keeps the benchmark honest.
Cost / risk
Loid edits one label before the bench is certified. That is free now and costly after B0.
Depends on

Measured by

C3–C18

Sixteen new usability tells

S each

Each tell is a mechanical or near-mechanical detector for one rule. The full YAML is in proposals.md §a. They are marked one by one below. The default cost and risk is false positives on clean screens, caught by F4 and the clean controls CC-01 to CC-08. Each is measured by recall and precision in its space (§2.3). Examples are dev bench items, Gnomon artifacts, or labelled illustrations.

C3 generic-error-copy · fail · rule errors-say-what-why-fix

Detects: "Something went wrong", "An error occurred", "contact your administrator". Why: NN/g error rubric; "contact admin" is a dead end (complex apps). Example: DE-03 Setup.tsx "Something went wrong" → "Couldn't reach the local model on :11434. Start it with gnomon local up, or pick Claude." Gnomon's refusal:v1 already has next:[{verb…}] and is the house pattern to follow.

C4 are-you-sure · warn · rule reversible-or-gated-proportionally

Detects: a generic "Are you sure?". Why: a gate should name the consequence (03 H5; user control and freedom). Example (illustrative): "Are you sure?" → "Uninstall mod workshop? Its 3 grants are revoked; rollback keeps 7 days."

C5 toast-for-error · fail · rule notification-matches-urgency

Detects: errors in auto-dismissing toasts. Why: indicators, validations, notifications: toasts are poor for errors. Example (illustrative): a Gnomon "session crashed" toast becomes an inbox card with re-attach.

C6 disabled-without-reason · warn · rule prevent-errors-with-constraints

Detects: disabled controls with no explanation. Why: 03 H5. Example (illustrative): a greyed "Approve" on a fresh-presence card → "Approve (needs Touch ID)".

C7 opaque-long-wait · warn · rule waits-show-honest-progress

Detects: a bare spinner or "Processing…" past 10 s. Why: 0.1 / 1 / 10 s limits. Example (illustrative): "⠋ Working…" → "3/7 steps · writing tests · Esc to stop".

C8 no-exit · fail · rule every-state-has-an-exit

Detects: modals, wizards or TUI modes with no visible exit. Why: 03 H3. Example: the Gnomon Workshop mod view must show its close or toggle key in the hint line.

C9 synonym-actions · warn · rule consistent-and-changes-look-changed

Detects: one action with several verbs. Why: 03 H4. Example: DE-05 AutomationsSheet has "Remove" and "Delete" for the same act → "Delete" in both places.

C10 icon-only-control · fail · rule semantic-structure-for-machines

Detects: an icon with no text, tooltip or accessible name. Why: agents as users, prompt controls. Example: DE-07 CommandPalette, where two commands share one glyph → unique glyphs, each labelled.

C11 internal-jargon · warn · rule speak-the-users-language

Detects: null, enum, snake_case keys, #symbol-ids in user copy. Why: 03 H2. Example: DE-06 NodeCard state: PENDING_MERGE → "Waiting to merge". Override: developer CLIs where the id is the input.

C12 color-only-state · fail · rule color-never-sole-signal

Detects: a state that disappears in grayscale or NO_COLOR. Why: WCAG 1.4.1; dashboards. Example: DE-04 VoiceSettings mic shown as a red or green dot → "● Listening" / "○ Muted". Gnomon caps: "filled = Claude, outlined = local" already passes because it uses shape.

C13 area-or-angle-chart · warn · rule quantities-by-length-or-position

Detects: Pie, Donut, Gauge, Radar, Treemap components. Why: length and position are read accurately; area and angle are not (NN/g, Cleveland & McGill). Example (illustrative): the Gnomon budget.meter block as a bar with a number, not a gauge.

C14 blank-prompt-box · warn · rule capabilities-and-limits-up-front

Detects: an AI input with no suggestions or capabilities. Why: articulation barrier, Wattenberger, Apple HIG GenAI. Example (illustrative): the Gnomon new-session prompt gets three chips from recent work ("resume Warpline console", "run tests", "what's pending?").

C15 generic-source-label · warn · rule citations-beside-claims

Detects: citations labelled "Source 1". Why: explainable AI: people rarely click, so the label must say what backs the claim. Example (illustrative): "[Source 2]" → "[gnomon.md §3.4 decision classes]".

C16 approve-default-focus · fail · rule decision-card-anatomy

Detects: Enter or initial focus mapped to Approve, Allow or Delete. Why: designing AI agents: permission needs an active choice. Example: DE-01 PermissionCards → focus on "Edit scope". This matters most for Gnomon's cached-unlock allow|always decisions, where one keystroke really commits.

C17 anthropomorphic-status · warn · rule explain-by-sources-not-narration

Detects: "I thought about", "I understand", "Pondering". It deliberately excludes "Thinking…". Why: NN/g explainable AI (opinion) and Turpin et al. 2023 (traces are not faithful). Example: DE-02 TranscriptRail. See G2 for the conflict with the benchmark label.

Link types

  • supports: a source supports a neuron.
  • contradicts: n:communication-not-decoration contradicts n:aesthetic-usability-effect. The pushbacks stay visible in the graph.
  • extends: n:tui-status-line extends n:visibility-of-system-status.
  • example-of: a Gnomon card is an example of n:decision-card-anatomy.
